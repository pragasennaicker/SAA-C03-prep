02 · DEEP DIVE

What you actually need to understand

1

HA vs fault tolerance vs DR

  • High availability minimizes downtime through redundant resources and automated failover.
  • Fault-tolerant designs continue operating despite component failures with minimal interruption.
  • Disaster recovery restores or shifts a workload after major failures such as a regional event.
  • A Multi-AZ design protects against AZ failure but is not automatically a complete regional DR strategy.
2

RPO + RTO

  • RPO is the maximum acceptable period of data loss measured backward from an incident.
  • RTO is the maximum acceptable time to restore service.
  • Lower RPO/RTO usually requires more replication, pre-provisioned capacity and automation.
  • The exam often gives RPO/RTO clues indirectly: 'no more than 5 minutes of data loss' is RPO.
3

DR strategies

  • Backup & restore: minimal standing infrastructure; lowest cost, longer recovery.
  • Pilot light: core data/services replicated and essential infrastructure kept ready; scale remaining components during recovery.
  • Warm standby: smaller but fully functional environment continuously running.
  • Multi-site active-active: multiple production environments serve traffic; fastest recovery but highest cost/complexity.
4

Data durability

  • Use service-native Multi-AZ replication for local availability.
  • Cross-Region replication/backup protects against regional failures according to service semantics.
  • Cross-account backups can reduce blast radius for destructive credentials/account compromise.
  • Test restore/failover procedures; untested backup plans are not reliable recovery plans.
5

Failure automation

  • Route 53 health/failover can switch DNS endpoints.
  • Auto Scaling replaces unhealthy compute; load balancers route around unhealthy targets.
  • Infrastructure as code accelerates reproducible recovery.
  • Service quotas in standby Regions must be sufficient before a disaster.

04 · WORKED SCENARIOS

Apply the concepts as an architect

WORKED SCENARIO

Recover a noncritical application

A system can lose up to 24 hours of data and remain offline for a day, while minimizing steady-state cost. Use cross-Region backups with infrastructure as code and a tested restore runbook. Warm standby pays for running capacity that the RTO does not require, and multi-site is far more expensive.

1 Long RPO→2 Long RTO→3 Lowest standby cost
WORKED SCENARIO

Keep a scaled-down recovery stack

A business service needs a minutes-level RTO and very low RPO in a second Region, but cannot fund full duplicate capacity. Run a warm standby with replicated data and reduced application capacity, then scale on failover. Pilot light may need too much provisioning time, while multi-site pays for full active capacity.

1 Stack already live→2 Scale on failover→3 Replicated data
WORKED SCENARIO

Serve from both Regions

A revenue platform requires near-zero RTO and RPO and can accept the highest operating cost and application complexity. Use multi-site active-active architecture with globally replicated data and health-based traffic steering. Backups, pilot light, and warm standby all require startup or scaling that violates the recovery objective.

1 Both sites active→2 Immediate routing→3 Highest readiness

→ RPO.

→ RTO.

→ Pilot light.
“both Regions serve users”
→ Active-active.
“protect from AZ failure”
→ Multi-AZ.
06 · KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Five SAA-style questions

Q1A workload may lose at most 15 minutes of committed data after a disaster. Which recovery metric states this requirement?
Answer: A. The deciding constraint is acceptable data loss measured backward from disruption. That is the recovery point objective.
Q2A company can tolerate a 24-hour outage and daily data loss, and it prioritizes the lowest ongoing DR cost. Which strategy is most suitable?
Answer: B. The deciding constraints are long RTO and RPO plus minimum cost. Backup and restore avoids continuously running a recovery environment.
Q3Core databases replicate to a second Region, but application servers are created only after disaster declaration. Which DR strategy does this most closely describe?
Answer: C. The deciding pattern is always-on core data infrastructure with application capacity provisioned during recovery. That is pilot light.
Q4A service requires near-zero recovery time and data loss across Regions, and cost is secondary. Which strategy best meets the objectives?
Answer: D. The deciding constraints are near-zero RTO and RPO. Multi-site keeps full service capability active and routes around a failed Region.
Q5An RDS Multi-AZ database survives an Availability Zone failure. Why might this still be insufficient for the stated Regional disaster requirement?
Answer: A. The deciding failure scope is an entire Region. Multi-AZ improves in-Region availability but does not by itself create a cross-Region recovery site.
